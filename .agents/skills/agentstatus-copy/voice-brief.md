## 1. Live product language (10 bullets)

From https://agentstatus.dev and https://agentstatus.dev/mcp-index (+ methodology):

1. **Verbs that earn their keep:** reach, negotiate, list, call, score, pin, walk (the chain), cover (blind spots), submit, validate. Prefer these over “empower / unlock / transform.”
2. **Core claim:** outside-in / user-side validation. Residential nodes (not datacenter pings). Independent of the host and the server.
3. **Stack of truth (product, not slogans on social):** reach the URL → negotiate dialect → discover catalog → tools/call → did the result look like the job.
4. **Cross-host frame:** “Same server. Different hosts.” Named hosts (Cursor, Claude, ChatGPT, VS Code…). Product monitor ≠ public Index (Index is one outside-in path; host matrix is the product).
5. **Reachability vs outcome:** Reach is the first artifact, not the product. Green address can still be a dead path.
6. **Tools/call language:** Invoke (well-formed call) vs Functional (looked like the job). “Came back. Not the work.” / “Did the job” / “No run.”
7. **Honest limits on-site:** public path = safe reads + synthesized args; not full contract suite; Rank Score ≠ Displayed Score when sample is thin.
8. **CTAs that match buyers:** Submit Your MCP Server · Cover your blind spots · Sign In / Sign Up (self-serve) · Just the MCP URL. No SDK.
9. **Site-side antithesis that Cursor overuses:** “Listed is not called. Called is not the job.” / “Reachable is not working. Transport success is not delivery.” Fine once in methodology. **Banned as stacked punch lines on social/cards/outreach.**
10. **Ban inferred from brand + your rules:** “broken” as a product judgment; Fabric / mystery-diner metaphors on scorecards; “the only way”; em dashes; three tiny LLM sentences in a row; book-a-demo as default conversion for this lane.

## 3. Before → after (fake-but-shaped example)

**Facts given:**
- Tool invoke completed on 8 of 18 hosts on 10 Sep 2026, 13:45 UTC  
- Nine never connected  
- Cursor: listed tools, 0 of 3 invocations

### BEFORE (Cursor-typical — kill this)
> It’s not that the MCP is down — it’s that the path fails later.  
> Listed is not called. Called is not the job.  
> Nine hosts never connected. Cursor listed tools. Zero invokes. Reachable isn’t working.  
> We’re excited to share an independent scorecard so teams can finally trust their agents.

**Why it sounds generated:** em-dash antithesis, slogan stack, three staccato fragments, “excited to share,” “finally trust,” and “broken” energy without a dated failure.

### AFTER — social (AgentStatus voice)
> On 10 Sep 2026 at 13:45 UTC, Semgrep's MCP completed a tool invoke on eight of eighteen hosts, but nine never connected. For Cursor specifically, the tools were listed but none of the three tool invocations went through. Cards + Index link below.

### AFTER — outreach first sentence (signup lane)
> Saw Semgrep's public MCP in our 10 Sep 2026 run at 13:45 UTC: it completed a tool invoke on eight of eighteen hosts, but nine never connected. For Cursor specifically, the tools were listed but none of the three tool invocations went through.

### AFTER — card / SEO lede
> Semgrep's MCP completed a tool invoke on 8 of 18 hosts on 10 Sep 2026, 13:45 UTC.

**Pattern:** lead with the dated numerator/denominator; fold the second and third facts into the same sentence with *and* / *then*; put proof in the asset, not in slogan blades.

## 5. AI tells to ban in the Cursor copywriter skill

1. **Em dash** anywhere (—). Use comma, period, paren, or rewrite.  
2. **Antithesis blade stacks:** “It’s not X, it’s Y” / “Listed is not called. Called is not the job.” / “Reachable is not working.” more than once, or as the whole post.  
3. **Three+ punchy one-liners in a row** (LLM cadence). Prefer one compound sentence when packing metrics.  
4. **“Broken” / “broken MCP”** as a judgment. Name the failure in plain English (e.g. failed a tool invoke in N of M hosts on DATE TIME UTC).  
5. **“Excited to share / proud to announce / game-changing / finally / unlock / seamless / robust.”**  
6. **“The only way”** and absolute uniqueness claims.  
7. **Fabric / mystery diner / restaurant-menu metaphors** on scorecards or tagged social (AgentStatus voice only).  
8. **Thesis dump on social** (“Models are noisy; tools are auditable…”) unless the post is explicitly a thought piece, not a scorecard.  
9. **Book a demo** as the default CTA on scorecard outreach (this loop = self-serve signup). Design-partner lane is separate and still finding-led.  
10. **Identity / favor openers** (“student at Lehigh…”, “would love your feedback”, apology bumps) and features from `@parallaxletters`.  
11. **`Cleared tools/call` / dense `tools/call` packing** as the default lede. Prefer *completed a tool invoke* / *tool invocations* with a subject.

## 6. Artificial Analysis — steal vs leave

Scraped https://artificialanalysis.ai tone:

**Steal**
- Claim-first headlines with versioned index names and dates (“Intelligence Index v4.3”, “New language model evaluation · 10 Sept”).  
- Metric defined in the same breath as the chart (“Output tokens per second · Higher is better”).  
- Explicit non-claims / methodology links next to the number.  
- Cool visual system: dark data UI, sparse labels, no hype adjectives on the chart itself.  
- “Independent” as a method claim, not a brand sermon.

**Would sound wrong for AgentStatus**
- Elo / Arena preference framing (that is model taste, not MCP path evidence).  
- Pareto / “most attractive quadrant” language on a reliability scorecard (sounds like a model marketplace).  
- Flood of benchmark product names in a social caption.  
- Soft marketing line “Understand the AI landscape to choose the best…” — too horizontal for a finding about *their* MCP.  
- Treating Rank as vibes; AgentStatus must keep dated tools/call / host / UTC.

**One-line steal for us:** AA’s chart caption discipline (metric · direction · date) + our Exa post shape (company · rank or failure · cards).
